## 3. D9 Field of Cloth (item b)

**3.1 What the board shows.** A numberless field of grey poles, each with one grey strip hanging straight down; one pale path; one newer foreground strip; ash-grey landscape (file 03 §1).

**3.2 Documented evidence.**
- **Death-announcement cloth on poles, one locality — DOCUMENTED (colonial-era, one observer, one locality), REGIONALLY SPECIFIC.** Basden (1921), *Among the Ibos of Nigeria*, p.117 (RP-64; full text read in part; p.117 verbatim-checked this cycle per RP-83): "As a general rule no signs are set up to indicate that death has visited a household, but in certain places — as at Awka — it is customary to hang strips of cloth, pennant-wise, on long poles, or to hang up a shirt or other garment belonging to the deceased to announce the fact that the owner thereof has passed away."
  - Limits: "in certain places" is the only scope the passage gives; period of display, number of poles and spacing are not stated; Basden says as a general rule no signs were set up; not dated as pre-colonial; not Igbo-wide; not a grave marker; not a field.
  - Attribution wording (mandatory for any Ogirishi-related note; for this item use): record it as Basden's colonial-era report for Awka only.
- **Funerary cloth on the body or as stand-in — DOCUMENTED, REGIONAL.** Corpse wrapped in grass mat and pieces of cloth on a stretcher (Basden 1921 p.116, RP-64). Udenu (Enugu State), cloth covering and jaw-tie (RP-32, Onuora 2016; body claim from snippet, not re-checked in full text). Nsukka folded George cloth standing for the corpse in *nvule onwu* (RP-31, Ikenga Journal; introduction, method and data read; peer-review status not confirmed). Onitsha and Nsukka cloth on the late husband (RP-34, Okorie; full text chunk 0 of 3 read). These are documented but regional and are funerary rites; do not reproduce the sequences (file 06).
- **Grave or memorial pole-and-cloth field:** NOT FOUND in the programme search (about 60 earlier searches and about 20 this cycle). Absence is not proof of non-existence. A systematic regional survey has not been done (U-03).
- **Sacred-tree white cloth (Akpu, Udara):** a secondary article (RP-04, Okeke 2022) gives this without a separate citation for those sentences. It is devotional and not funerary. It is NOT of equal evidence strength to the corpse-covering evidence above and must not be presented as a funerary parallel.
- **Ogirishi grave-headside and boundary tree:** a secondary article reports it. The original source and locality remain unverified. Mandatory wording: "A secondary source reports an Ogirishi tree associated with grave headsides and boundaries; the original source and locality remain unverified." This item is not evidence for the Field of Cloth.

**3.4 Chapter 7 implications.**
- Chapter 7 is PROPOSED, not canon. The central burial-cloth reading has no documented pole analogue (research file 04, Chapter 7 summary).
- Basden's Awka precedent changes one point: a death-announcement form on poles now exists in the sources. It supports the question of a death-sign element. It does not support a field, a grave marker or an Igbo-wide funerary landscape. The 'NOT FOUND' statements in RESEARCH/B, C, E and A are superseded for the death-announcement case only (file 03 §10).
- Dossier 'NOT FOUND' statements for grave, field or memorial poles stand.

**3.5 Research position on D9.** Keep HOLD. Research does not clear D9. Desk research cannot clear a consultant-gated element. The board must not be captioned as an Igbo funerary marker. If a cloth-and-death element is kept, the documented forms are (a) cloth covering, tying or carrying the body, or (b) cloth standing in for the dead in procession. Both are regional and unlike the board's hung strips. Consultation is required before finalising any such element; do not depict the corpse.

## 4. VR-011 Landing of the Unclaimed (H3)

- **Cloth-wrapped bundle in canoe:** a cloth-wrapped body is documented (Basden p.116, grass mat and pieces of cloth; RP-64). The same passage says untimely deaths, including drowning, are buried without delay and not rubbed with camwood. A cloth-wrapped bundle in a canoe is therefore a documented wrapping form only in a bad-death context, which is restricted (U-15). The CD must decide whether the cargo may read as a body. Research cannot clear it.
- **Rampart strips on poles:** Awka pennant-strips are a temporary death-announcement form at one locality. They are not documented at a landing or as memorial strips for the unclaimed. Reading them as burial cloth remains SPECULATIVE (U-41).
- **Riverine canoe use:** DOCUMENTED for Onitsha and Aboh (RP-69; RP-64 Ch. I). Canoe count in the brief is unsourced (INFERRED / FICTIONAL in scale).
- **Oru-Igbo ferrying rite (RP-33):** REGIONALLY SPECIFIC, restricted detail, interpretation by the authors. Relevant only as a regional symbol. Either this reading or the bad-death reading would make the landing a funerary scene. That is a CD decision. If chosen, it must be attributed to the named region and consulted.
- **Research position:** keep HOLD.

## 5. Offering-place and washing-place dependencies

- **Documented practice (RP-64 Ch. IV, Basden 1921, pp.45–48):** water often distant; villagers dig catch-pits for surface water; no sanitation. No washing place, wall-base outlet or washing shrine is described. The documented practice is shallow surface collection.
- **Idemili and Ala** are documented as water and earth deities (RP-08, RP-10, RP-11; T3). Washing-place practice and any water-spirit shrine, taboo or offering at a washing place are NOT documented (U-27, OPEN).
- **Impluvium** (RP-73): reported by secondary sources for Igbo compounds; UNVERIFIED for Igbo (U-43).
- **VR-010 V005 APPROVED WITH QUALIFICATION:** domestic washing area only; NOT a ritual or offering location. Research notes that the approved panel's wall-base outlet is a visible open channel, consistent with the file-09 direction; that is an observation of the brief, not an approval. The U-27 qualification must be carried into any caption or brief.
- **VR-010 V003 rejected:** enclosed stone-lined recess reads as an offering niche. Rejection is consistent with research file 09 §7.2.
- **Ritual water (EXP-005 Still Pool):** REVISE. Built circular stone basin has no documented source. Keep the qualification.

## 9. Limits
- Most sources were read in part or as snippets (see §8).
- Colonial-era accounts may reflect colonial reporting.
- "Igbo" is not one practice. Udenu, Umumba-Ndiagu, Nsukka, Onitsha, Oru-Igbo and Awka are different localities.
- Absence of evidence is not proof of non-existence.
- No source is infallible. Desk research does not replace living custodians. No backward projection of modern practice is made.
