# Board Element → Evidence Map

**Scope:** Each element of the review board (`REVIEW_B4W_03_FIELD_OF_CLOTH_SIDE_BY_SIDE.jpg`) and what the evidence says about it. The map does **not** make design decisions. It shows which claims bear on each element and which questions remain open.

**Visual issues on the board** (poles sparse versus "numberless"; strips thin and hard to see) are visual-design matters and are **not** cultural-review questions. They are listed for completeness only. The research has no cultural evidence that bears on them, and no decision is made here.

---

## Element 1 — Ash-grey, vast, austere landscape

- **Board text:** "vast, austere" landscape; seven grey palette swatches.
- **Evidence:** No documented Igbo funerary colour convention for grey was found. White and black appear in some mourning contexts (C-02, C-29).
- **Claims:** C-02, C-29 (black cloth on a titled man's corpse, and widowhood dress); C-01 (grey not found as funerary colour).
- **Open questions:** F-09 (is grey used in any funerary or memorial context?), F-07 (what colour covers a corpse?).

## Element 2 — Numerous individual poles

- **Board text:** "Death has no hierarchy... each with one strip"; known issue "poles sparse (should feel numberless)".
- **Evidence:** Shrine figures and house posts are documented (C-11, C-34). A field of many poles is not documented as a funerary form (C-01). The Nsude shrines are stepped mud pyramids, not poles, and carry no cloth (C-33).
- **Claims:** C-01, C-11, C-33.
- **Open questions:** F-01 (are upright poles or posts used with cloth?), F-03 (do any poles mark a grave or a boundary?), F-06 (would a field of many poles be recognised?).

## Element 3 — One grey cloth strip hanging from each pole

- **Board text:** "One strip per pole, hanging straight down."; "Burial imagery needs consultant review. It copies no real grave marker."
- **Evidence:** Cloth in funerary contexts is reported as corpse covering and grave goods (C-02, C-05). White cloth on the trunks of some sacred trees is reported in one secondary article (C-08; Akpu and Udara named). Cult apparatus on Awka shrine walls is reported (C-11); cloth is not named. No documented pole-hung cloth marker was found (C-01). Pole and flag comparisons from other West African traditions are noted: Cape Coast asafo flags (C-30, scope limited) and Yoruba white-cloth trees (C-10). Vodun and Bongo comparisons are unverified (C-31, C-32). None of these is Igbo.
- **Claims:** C-01, C-02, C-05, C-08, C-10, C-11, C-30 to C-32.
- **Open questions:** F-01 to F-05 (pole and tree use), F-07 to F-10 (corpse cloth, grave goods, colour, term), F-36 (textiles for funerary use).

## Element 4 — No cloth strung between poles

- **Board text:** "Never strung in lines."
- **Evidence:** No documented Igbo rule either requires or forbids stringing cloth between posts. The rule is an artistic constraint in the source material. (No claim in the package.)
- **Open question:** F-39 (which features would remain sensitive if burial-cloth elements were removed?).

## Element 5 — Single pale path

- **Board text:** "A single pale path."
- **Evidence:** Some communities describe a journey or an ancestral world beneath the earth (general; not a documented route). No documented path form was found. (No specific claim; treated as inferred.)
- **Open question:** F-18 (is there a journey to the afterlife, and is its route restricted?).

## Element 6 — One newer-looking foreground strip

- **Board text:** "One strip in the foreground is newer than the rest."; "Death has no hierarchy."
- **Evidence:** Fresh cloth on a corpse or grave is reported (C-02). No documented pole-hung newer strip marking a recent death was found (C-01).
- **Claims:** C-01, C-02.
- **Open questions:** F-11 (does a fresh cloth on a grave signal a recent death?), F-12 (would one newer strip be read as recent grief?), F-13 (would a newest cloth be read as a real family or grave?).

## Element 7 — "Gates of the Dead" / land of the dead atmosphere

- **Board text:** "THE GATES OF THE DEAD"; footer "This is the game's invented land of the dead and makes no statement about Igbo belief."
- **Evidence:** Ancestral worlds and death classes are documented but vary by community (C-25, C-26, C-28). A secondary source reports an Ogirishi tree associated with grave headsides and boundaries; the original source and locality remain unverified. [C-09] It is a tree, not a gate or a pole. No single fixed Igbo land of the dead is documented.
- **Claims:** C-09, C-25, C-26, C-28.
- **Open questions:** F-14 (is a fictional land of the dead acceptable?), F-15 (which features must never be shown?), F-16 (would the title be read as a real place or ritual?), F-17 (what is the local term for the afterlife?), F-44 (tree grave and boundary markers).

## Element 8 — Overall composition and footer disclaimer

- **Board text:** "Entirely fictional... makes no statement about Igbo belief. Design PROPOSED, not canon."
- **Evidence:** The disclaimer is a statement by the studio. The research cannot establish whether a footer travels with an image in use, or whether a viewer would read a fictional scene as a statement about Igbo belief.
- **Open questions:** F-40 (what wording or disclaimer would your community require?), F-41 (who else should be consulted before a funerary image is used, even in fiction?).
