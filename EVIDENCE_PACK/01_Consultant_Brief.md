# Consultant Brief — Field of Cloth (CH07-SUB-005)

## 1. Who we are and why we are asking

THE DISOBEDIENT DAUGHTER is a game set in an Igbo-inspired historical-fantasy world. The studio has a review step, D9, which is blocked until an Igbo cultural consultant has reviewed one environment concept. We are asking you to review it, and to tell us what you know that published sources cannot settle.

We are not asking you to approve artwork. Creative decisions belong to the Creative Director. Our desk research is attached so you can check it, correct it, and tell us where it is wrong.

## 2. The concept under review

Concept ID: `ENV_GATES_DEAD_FIELD_OF_CLOTH_V001`. Register CH07-SUB-005, status PROPOSED, review required.

The review board's own text, quoted exactly:

- Title: "THE GATES OF THE DEAD — THE FIELD OF CLOTH"
- Read: "Death has no hierarchy... each with one strip of grey cloth hanging still. One strip in the foreground is newer than the rest. A single pale path."
- Rules: "One strip per pole, hanging straight down. Never strung in lines."
- Sensitivity: "Burial imagery needs consultant review. It copies no real grave marker."
- Known issues: "poles sparse (should feel numberless)"; "strips thin/hard to see on pale"
- Footer: "Entirely fictional. This is the game's invented land of the dead and makes no statement about Igbo belief. Design PROPOSED, not canon."

In plain terms: a wide grey landscape with many upright poles. Each pole carries one grey cloth strip hanging straight down. No cloth is strung between poles. One strip in the foreground looks newer than the others. A pale path crosses the field. The scene is meant to be a fictional land of the dead.

The footer says the scene is fictional. We recognise that a footer does not control how the image is read, and that the game will be read through its Igbo-inspired setting. That is one reason we are asking.

## 3. What the research found (summary)

Each statement has a source code. Full details are in `02_Evidence_Claims_Register.md`.

- **No documented pre-colonial Igbo practice** was found in which cloth strips hang individually from free-standing poles as a funerary, mourning or memorial marker. The search covered about 55–60 queries and cannot prove absence. [C-01]
- **Cloth in Igbo funerary contexts** is reported as grave goods and corpse covering in at least one community account: black cloth covers the body of a titled (Ozo) man, and cloth is measured in fathoms. This comes from a single 2025 article that reproduces a passage found in earlier work. It has not yet been confirmed against the original 1998 study. [C-02, C-03, C-05]
- **Two-chamber grave at Obimo** (Nsukka area) is reported in the same lineage of sources. [C-04]
- **White cloth on the trunks of some sacred trees** is reported in some Igbo localities. It is devotional, not funerary. [C-08]
- **A tree (Ogirishi) reported as marking grave headsides and boundaries** is reported in one secondary account. Its locality is not stated. [C-09]
- **White cloth on Awka-area shrine walls** with cult apparatus is reported in a standard art-historical survey. [C-11]
- **Pole-and-cloth arrangements** are associated with other West African traditions (for example Fante asafo flags in Ghana, and cloth-wrapped trees in Yoruba and vodun contexts). These are not Igbo, and the research makes no claim about them beyond noting them. [C-10, C-30 to C-32]
- **Igbo-Ukwu textiles** are identified as bast fibre from fig (*Ficus*) species and leaf fibre from *Raphia* palm. No silk was reported. [C-12]

Several sources are reported through other people's work or through search snippets. Those are flagged. Please treat the summary above as a set of leads to check, not as settled fact.

## 4. What we are asking you

The full question list is in `05_Consultant_Response_Sheet.csv` (questions 1 to 46). The most important are:

1. Whether cloth strips are ever hung from upright poles or posts in your community, and where. (Q1–Q3)
2. What cloth is used to cover a corpse or is placed with the dead in your community, and whether colour or quantity varies by status. (Q7–Q10)
3. Whether a fresh cloth on or near a grave has any meaning in your community, and whether a newer strip in a scene would be read as a real death. (Q11–Q13)
4. Whether a fictional land of the dead is acceptable to depict in a game, and which features, if any, must never be shown. (Q14–Q18)
5. Which practices are restricted or sensitive, and who may describe or depict them. (Q19–Q25)
6. Which sensitive subjects (bad death, twins, children, widowhood) need extra care. (Q26–Q30)
7. Which community or communities your knowledge covers, and where your answers do not apply to others. (Q31–Q35)

You may decline any question. Please say so in the response sheet rather than guessing.

## 5. What we are not asking

- We are not asking you to design, approve or reject any visual element.
- We are not asking you to speak for all Igbo people. Please tell us which community or communities your answer covers.
- We are not asking for restricted knowledge to be published. Please tell us what may be recorded, and what may be published, separately.

## 6. Handling and consent

- **Restricted knowledge:** please mark any answer as restricted in the response sheet. Restricted answers will not be used in public-facing material.
- **Recording and publication:** the response sheet has separate columns for "may record" and "may publish". Please complete both. If you are unsure, mark "no" and we will ask.
- **Attribution:** we will credit you only in the way you specify. Tell us your preferred name or reference, or ask us to anonymise you.
- **Disagreement:** if other custodians or sources disagree with you, tell us. We would rather keep the disagreement than force a single answer.
- **Terms of engagement** (fee, timing, format): not set out in this brief. The production team will agree these with you directly.

## 7. What happens with your answers

Your answers are an input to the Creative Director's review. They do not decide the outcome. We will share the consolidated answers, with your restrictions respected, before any decision on the concept.

## 8. Limits of this brief

- The research package was produced by desk research on 2026-11-17. It was not produced with community input.
- Some sources are colonial-era records or secondary summaries. Their limits are noted in `03_Source_Verification_Log.md`.
- Where this brief describes a practice, it describes what a source reports. It does not establish that the practice is current, or that it is shared by other communities.
