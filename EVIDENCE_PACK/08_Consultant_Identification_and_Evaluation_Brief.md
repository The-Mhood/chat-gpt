## 2. Expertise profiles (categories to search for)

No profile below is a person or an institution. Each is a category. A candidate fits a profile only after verification (§4).

**P1. Community custodian (primary).** A person recognised by their own community as holding knowledge of funerary, shrine or ancestral practice, usually through a titled or lineage role. Covers Q1–Q25, Q39–Q46 for their community. This is the most important profile. Desk research cannot replace it.

**P2. Community custodian for women's and widowhood practice.** Recognised knowledge holder for widowhood, mourning and women's institutions in their community. Covers Q29–Q30 and parts of Q26–Q28. Engage only with the community's agreement and any protocol it sets.

**P3. Ethnographer or social anthropologist with recent fieldwork in south-eastern Nigeria.** Able to say which practices are documented across communities, which are local, and where the research record is thin. Useful for Q31–Q35. Not a substitute for P1.

**P4. Regional archaeologist or historian, including the Nsukka region.** Able to address Igbo-Ukwu dating and identity (Q36–Q38), Nsukka-region material (the acknowledged gap in A, Part VIII), and historical forms of memorialisation. Useful for Q31–Q35 and Q44–Q45.

**P5. Textile and fibre specialist (African textiles).** Able to assess the fibre identifications and textile claims in SPR22 and the Igbo textile sources, and to say what cannot be inferred from two samples. Covers Q36–Q38.

**P6. Igbo-language and terminology specialist.** Able to answer the term questions (for example Q10: what a grave cloth is called, and whether "burial cloth" is an acceptable term for the consultant's community). Also checks the spelling and use of Igbo terms in the pack.

**P7. Cultural-heritage advisor or practising Igbo artist with community standing (secondary).** Able to advise on the fictional framing, disclaimer wording, and the difference between a respectful fictional setting and an implied real practice (Q14–Q18, Q39–Q43). This profile supports P1; it does not replace the custodian's view on sacred or funerary content.

**Not a consultant profile.** Museum collection staff, journal editors, or authors of the secondary sources in G are useful for source checks (§7), but they are not treated as cultural custodians for the purposes of D9 unless they also meet P1–P4.

## 4. Identification method

**Channels (in order of preference):**
1. **Trusted intermediaries:** community contacts, cultural bodies or academic departments already known to the TD or studio, who can make an introduction. The introducer should not be paid on a success basis.
2. **Academic routes:** authors of the sources in G who are publicly listed and whose community standing is independently checkable. Check first whether the author is a P1 custodian or a P3/P4 scholar before approaching them about community knowledge.
3. **Institutional routes:** university departments of anthropology, archaeology, history or Igbo studies in south-eastern Nigeria. Use institutional contact points, not personal social media.

**Rules:**
- **No cold approach on restricted topics.** Do not describe restricted items, or the consultation's sensitive questions, in an unsolicited first message. The first message says who the studio is, that the work is a desk-research review of a fictional game concept, and that the consultant may decline. It does not ask about restricted practice.
- **Community gatekeepers first where the community has one.** For P1 and P2 candidates, ask whether the community has a process for outside requests. Follow it. Do not bypass a gatekeeper to reach an individual.
- **Verify credentials independently.** Do not rely on search snippets, self-description or the research package's own sources to confirm who someone is.
- **Record conflicts of interest.** Note any financial or commercial link to the game, to the studio, or to a competing product, and any role in a dispute over the practices in question.
- **Do not select for a preferred answer.** A candidate who will only confirm the concept is a warning sign, not a success.
- **Do not present the pack before consent.** The first contact does not include the research package.

## 5. Evaluation criteria

Each candidate is assessed against the criteria below. The assessment records **fitness to be consulted**. It does not record approval of any artwork or any clearance.

| Criterion | What good looks like | Red flags |
|---|---|---|
| Community standing | Recognised by the community as knowledgeable on the topic; can say what they can and cannot speak for | Speaks for "all Igbo"; self-described expert with no community recognition |
| Scope match | Covers the community or topic the question needs (see §3) | Covers a region the studio does not need, or nothing the studio needs |
| Topic competence | Knows the funerary, shrine or restricted domain in question, and where their knowledge stops | Confident on everything; no stated limits |
| Language | Speaks Igbo (and the relevant dialect) or works with a trusted interpreter | Requires the studio to rely on a machine translation of a restricted answer |
| Independence | No financial stake in the game; no commercial link to the studio's competitors | Paid by the studio to produce a favourable answer |
| Restriction awareness | Knows which items are restricted and will say so without describing them | Offers restricted detail freely in first contact |
| Disagreement | Willing to record disagreement with other custodians or sources | Requires a single answer to be recorded |
| Availability and conditions | Can commit to the review; has stated conditions for reviewing a revised image (Q42) | Unavailable for the timeline; no conditions stated |
| Consent process | Accepts written consent, separate may-record and may-publish answers, and withdrawal | Refuses written consent, or wants unrecorded answers used in publication |

**Outcome categories (for the TD's record):**
- **Proceed to consent conversation:** meets P1 or the relevant profile; no red flags.
- **Proceed with limits:** meets the profile for some questions only. Record the limits; do not ask the other questions.
- **Do not proceed:** a red flag that cannot be resolved, or a community gatekeeper declines.

## 6. Scope of the consultation

**Ask:**
- The open questions in F (§1), starting with the priority items: Q1–Q6, Q7–Q10, Q11–Q13 and Q44–Q46, and the consent and condition questions Q39–Q43.
- For Section 5 (restricted items), ask only whether an item is restricted and who may depict it. Do not ask for detail.

**Do not ask:**
- For the artwork to be approved, rejected or redesigned. The consultant is not asked for creative decisions (F, Note to the production team; brief §5).
- For restricted ritual detail beyond what is needed to identify the consultation boundary. The consultation boundary is the item name, whether it is restricted, and who may depict it. Ritual procedure, names, verse, objects and locations are not asked for.
- For any statement about "all Igbo" practice.

**Review of a revised image (Q42).** A revised concept image is not in this package. If a consultant agrees to review one, a separate request is needed, with its own consent and its own scope. No image is shared before that consent.

## 7. Consent, recording and restriction

These rules apply to every consultation. They follow the response-sheet columns (`05_Consultant_Response_Sheet.csv`) and the brief §6.

1. **Written, informed consent before any substantive answer.** The consultant is told: what the game is; what the concept is; that the research is desk research; who will see the answers; and that they may decline any question.
2. **Separate permissions.** Record separately: whether the answer may be **recorded** (`may_record`); whether it may be **published** (`may_publish`); and whether the consultant is **declined** on any question (`declined`). If the consultant is unsure, the answer is recorded as "no" until the consultant confirms.
3. **Restricted answers.** Mark any restricted answer as `restricted = yes`. Restricted answers are not used in public-facing material, and not in consultant-facing summaries that go to others, without the consultant's **written permission** (F, Note to the production team).
4. **Recording is optional.** A consultant may answer in writing only. Audio or video recording requires explicit consent for each session.
5. **Withdrawal.** The consultant may withdraw an answer, or their whole participation, at any time before publication. Withdrawal removes the answer from all working files and from any summary the studio has prepared. The studio records that withdrawal occurred, not the content.
6. **Attribution.** Credit only in the way the consultant specifies (name, reference, or anonymous). Record the chosen form in `consultant_reference_or_name`.
7. **Disagreement is kept.** Where consultants or sources disagree, record both (F, Note to the production team).
8. **Data handling.** Store consent records and answers under the studio's data-protection policy. Restrict access to the TD and the research team. Keep restricted answers in a separate, access-controlled location. This document does not set the technical arrangement; the TD must confirm it.
9. **Interpreters.** If an interpreter is used, the interpreter also agrees to the restriction and confidentiality terms, and is recorded in the consent file.
10. **Children and vulnerable persons.** Questions on child burial, bad death and widowhood are asked only where the community considers them appropriate to discuss (Q46 wording). Do not record a person's account of a child's death without the consultant's and, where the community requires it, the family's agreement.
11. **Community protocol outranks the studio's request.** If a community asks that a question not be discussed, the question is withdrawn and the withdrawal is recorded.

## 8. Compensation considerations (for TD decision; no amounts set here)

The brief (§6) states that terms are "not set out in this brief" and are for the production team to agree directly with each consultant. This section lists the points the TD should decide before first contact. It does not set rates.

- **Pay for time, not for an answer.** Honoraria should not depend on whether the answer supports the concept. A payment that depends on approval is a conflict of interest and should not be made.
- **Cover costs.** Transport, communication, interpreting and any time away from work should be covered as separate items, agreed in advance.
- **Who receives payment.** Ask the consultant (and, where a community protocol applies, the community) how payment should be made and to whom. Do not assume that payment to an individual is the community's preferred form.
- **Hospitality and recognition norms.** Ask the consultant what is customary. Do not impose a studio norm in place of the community's.
- **Written terms.** Agree the scope, the fee, the dates, the consent terms and the attribution in writing before work begins.
- **Recognition in the game.** Any credit, acknowledgement or sign-off is agreed with the consultant. A consultant who has reviewed a concept is not to be named as approving it unless they have agreed to that wording.
- **Unpaid advice is not offered as a substitute.** Do not ask for consultation on a voluntary basis to avoid paying for the time.

**Decision needed:** the TD sets the budget envelope, the payment method and the approval route for terms before first contact.

## 10. Outstanding evidence limitations

These limitations are carried forward from the correction manifest (`07_Correction_Manifest_v1.1.md`, §11.6–11.7). They must be stated to any consultant who asks, and they limit what the studio can say before the consultation ends.

**Sources not read in full:**
- **SPR22** (McIntosh & Cartwright 2022, Igbo-Ukwu textiles): abstract only. Whether the AMS date applies to the Igbo Isaiah objects is not stated in the abstract.
- **OKE22** (Okeke 2022): chunks 1–3 re-read in the v1.1-final pass; chunks 0, 4 and 5 not re-read. No page numbers in the fetched text.
- **IGI25** (Chinweze 2025): author, title and journal retrieved; full text not re-read.
- **UGWU98** (Ugwuoke 1998, Obimo): not retrieved. The Obimo two-chamber detail rests on secondary reporting.
- **ADI08** (Adibe 2008): not retrieved. The white-cloth sentences in the Akpu and Udara paragraphs carry no separate citation in OKE22, so their primary source is unknown.
- **AID11** (Aidoo 2011, Fante asafo flags): repository record and abstract only. Pole mounting and funerary use are not established.
- **BJMCMR21, ETE:** reproduce the Obimo passage; not independent of IGI25.

**Claims unresolved or unverified:**
- The **Ogirishi** grave and boundary statement is unsourced in OKE22. The original source and locality are unknown. The mandated wording is used in every file that makes the claim.
- **Iroko** and its restricted association are recorded in C-47 as an internal note only. Not discussed with consultants.
- **Twelve fathoms** for a father's burial cloth is a secondary figure, not confirmed in retrieved text.
- **Ukara and Ekpe** attribution to Igbo is unverified.
- **Igbo-Ukwu** dating and identity are DISPUTED. The two-sample limit applies.
- **Bongo grave posts** and **vodun cloth-wrapped trees** have no scholarly source in the package. They are not Igbo.
- **Asafo** (Fante): one MA thesis, scope limited to Cape Coast flag documentation.
- **Ogene and ekwe:** Tier 4 sources only; pre-colonial origin not established.

**Coverage gaps:**
- **Nsukka region** archaeology and funerary practice: not reviewed in depth (A, Part VIII).
- **Idemili and other river-deity contexts:** not reviewed in depth.
- **Child burial treatment:** GAP.
- **Pole, post and flag use across south-eastern Nigeria:** no systematic search.

**Register and pack gaps (TD decisions):**
- **C-23, C-33, C-40** are "Not covered by the current question list" and have no consultant question. TD to decide whether to add questions.
- **F-10** (grave-cloth terminology) has no linked claim.
- **20 CSV rows** have no linked claim ("none"). Recorded in the sheet.

**Process gaps:**
