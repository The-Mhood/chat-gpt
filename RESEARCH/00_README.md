## Conventions

**Evidence labels (mandatory):** DOCUMENTED · CORROBORATED · REGIONALLY SPECIFIC · DISPUTED · INFERRED · SPECULATIVE · FICTIONAL

**Period tags:** [PRE-COL] pre-colonial documentation · [EARLY-COL] early colonial encounter (c. 1880s–1910s) · [COL-DEV] developed or changed under colonial rule · [CONTEMP] contemporary practice · [ORIGIN-UNCERTAIN]

**Source codes** (e.g., `[BAS21]`) map to `G_Bibliography.md`. Each code carries a reliability tier:
- Tier 1: primary archaeological or original ethnographic record
- Tier 2: peer-reviewed or university-press scholarship
- Tier 3: reputable institutions and archives
- Tier 4: lead only — blogs, news, commercial or community sites. Not used as the basis for any historical conclusion.

**Verification flags:** Where a citation was recovered from a secondary web summary and full bibliographic details could not be confirmed in this session, it is marked **[VERIFY]**. Page numbers are given only where a source reported them.

## Single most important finding

No documented pre-colonial Igbo practice was found in which cloth strips hang individually from free-standing poles as a funerary, mourning or memorial marker. This is a finding about the search record (55–60 searches, count approximate across the sources available), not proof that no such practice existed in any locality. The nearest documented Igbo practices are different in form and meaning (see Deliverable B, Section 6).

Pole-and-cloth arrangements are a well-known feature of other West African traditions (for example, Fante asafo flags in Ghana). They must not be attributed to Igbo culture.
