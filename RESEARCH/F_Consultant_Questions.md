# Deliverable F — Questions for a Qualified Igbo Cultural Consultant
THE DISOBEDIENT DAUGHTER · Field of Cloth (CH07-SUB-005) and related imagery · Working date 2026-11-17

> **Who to ask:** a living Igbo cultural custodian with knowledge of the specific community or communities involved. Ideally more than one, from different regions (for example, an Awka-area or Anambra custodian, an Owerri-area custodian, and a Nsukka-area custodian), because practices differ.
>
> **Why these questions:** published research cannot settle them. Several topics are restricted, and some sources are colonial-era or unverified.
>
> **Please do not share restricted answers beyond the consultation.** Ask the consultant what may be recorded or published.

---

## Section 1 — Pole and cloth imagery (the core question)

1. In your community, are strips of cloth ever hung from upright poles or posts? If yes: where (compound entrance, shrine, grave, farm, junction, boundary), in what number, and at what time?
2. If a pole or post is used with cloth in your community, who is allowed to see it, touch it, or change it?
3. Are there any poles with cloth that mark a grave, a boundary between the living and the dead, or a place where the dead are believed to pass? If yes, what is the name of the object, and who is responsible for it?
4. If a single tree has white cloth wrapped around its trunk in your area, what is that tree's role? Is it restricted, and may outsiders describe or depict it?
5. Are there any locations in your community where many cloths are displayed together outdoors? If so, what is their purpose?
6. Is a field of many poles with grey cloth strips something your community would recognise, or something it would find unfamiliar or disturbing?

## Section 2 — Colour, cloth and burial

7. What cloth is used to cover a corpse in your community? Is it black, white, grey, or another colour? Does the colour change with the person's status, age or cause of death?
8. Are cloths buried with the dead or placed on a grave? If so, how many, what kind, and what do they signify? Is the quantity measured in fathoms, and does it vary with title?
9. Is grey cloth used in any funerary, mourning or memorial context? If not, what colour is used?
10. What do you call a grave cloth or grave covering? Is there a word that should be used instead of "burial cloth" when we describe these objects?

## Section 3 — A newer cloth

11. If a fresh cloth is placed on or near a grave, does it signal a recent death, a particular person, the date of burial, or a stage of the rites? Who places it, and when?
12. Would a single newer strip among older strips be read in your community as a sign of recent grief, a particular person's death, or something else?
13. Would the newest cloth in a scene be understood as a claim about a real family or real grave?

## Section 4 — Fictional land of the dead

14. Is it acceptable in your community to depict an invented "land of the dead" in a fictional game set in an Igbo-inspired world, provided the game states it is fictional and makes no claim about Igbo belief?
15. Are there any specific features of the land of the dead in your community that must not be shown in any form (e.g., a gate, a boundary, a path, a river, a field)?
16. Would the title "The Gates of the Dead" or "Field of Cloth" be understood as a reference to a real place or ritual in your community?
17. Is the phrase "land of the dead" acceptable in your language, and what term would you use for the afterlife or the world of the ancestors?
18. Does the afterlife in your community involve a journey? If so, is the route or landscape restricted, and may it be depicted?

## Section 5 — Restricted and sensitive items

19. Is the ikenga, or any personal carved object, ritually buried or broken with its owner in your community? Who may describe or depict it?
20. Are ichi scarifications and ozo title regalia restricted in your community? If so, which parts may be depicted, and which may not?
21. Are ogo, odo/omabe, mmonwu, okoroshi or other masquerades restricted in your community? Who may see or depict them?
22. Are mbari houses, shrine figures, or ala-related wall decorations restricted in your community?
23. Are nsibidi signs, ukara cloth or Ekpe objects known or used in your community? Are they restricted?
24. Is uli painting used on shrine walls in your community, and are the designs restricted? Which designs are safe to adapt, and which are not?
25. Does your community consider the depiction of death, a corpse, a grave or a burial rite as a sensitive subject in art or game media?

## Section 6 — Bad death, twins, ogbanje and children

26. In your community, what counts as a "bad death" (ajo ofia)? Would depicting such a death be considered offensive?
27. Are twins, ogbanje children or children who died young treated in a way that should not be depicted in an entertainment product?
28. What is the current view in your community of the osu status? Should it be referenced, and how?

## Section 7 — Widowhood and mourning

29. Which widowhood practices are current in your community, which have been abandoned, and which are contested? Should widowhood be shown at all in this project?
30. If widowhood practices are shown, what framing would be respectful, and what would be considered exploitative or spectacular?

## Section 8 — Regional and historical specificity

31. Which community or communities does your knowledge cover? Which practices in this list apply to your community, and which do not?
32. Did your community's funerary practice change under colonial rule or missionary influence? Which elements of the funerary practice have been lost or transformed?
33. Are there differences between your community's funerary practice and neighbouring communities that a viewer should understand before seeing an Igbo-inspired image?
34. Are the second burial (ikwa ozu or the local term) and its timing consistent with the accounts you have heard? What is the local term?
35. Is a titled man's compound burial, and the Obimo-type two-chamber grave, practised in your community? If not, what do you practise?

## Section 9 — Materials and textiles

36. Which textiles would be recognised in your community for funerary use? Which are restricted to particular people?
37. Is any textile in your community associated with silk, or is silk entirely outside local tradition?
38. Which cloth colours, weaves and patterns are restricted to title, age, gender or ceremony?

## Section 10 — Conditions for use

39. If the Field of Cloth concept were revised to remove burial-cloth elements, which elements would still be sensitive?
40. What wording, credit or disclaimer would your community require on any product that uses Igbo-inspired material?
41. Are there persons or families who should be consulted before a funerary image is used, even in fiction?
42. Would you be willing to review a revised concept image (not in this package) and advise? What are your conditions for doing so?
43. What should we not do even if the research (this package) suggests a practice is documented?

---

## Section 11 — Added after evidence verification (2026-11-17)

44. Is a tree used to mark a grave, the head of a grave, or a boundary in your community? One secondary report describes the Ogirishi tree as marking grave headsides and boundaries, and the graves of unmarried men. Is this recognised locally, and is any part of it restricted?
45. Is the two-chamber grave reported for Obimo (Nsukka area) recognised in your community or in neighbouring communities? If so, what are the chambers used for?
46. Is the practice reported in one 2025 account, of leaving the corpse of an unmarried man or a child in the evil forest, recognised in your community? Discuss only if your community considers it appropriate to discuss.

## Note to the production team

- Record the consultant's name, community, date, and consent conditions. Do not publish restricted answers without written permission.
- Where consultants disagree, keep their disagreement in the record. Do not choose one answer without noting the other.
- A consultant's answer applies to their community or region, not to all Igbo people.
- Desk research is not a substitute for these answers.
